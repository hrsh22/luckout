# Keep Bitrefill credit dynamic and refill it from a treasury

Every real card is paid from our USD store credit at Bitrefill (ADR-0001, `docs/research/bitrefill-funding.md`). That credit delivers codes in about a second, but Bitrefill never refunds it, so any credit we hold beyond what sales use is stranded. Paying each order directly in USDC instead was considered and rejected: delivery would take minutes instead of seconds, each card would cost about 1% more, and there would be more ways for an order to fail.

So the server keeps only as much credit as recent sales need and refills it by itself:

- **How much.** The target is two days of real-card spending (the last 7 days' average), between a floor and a ceiling an admin sets. A refill starts when credit drops under half the target and tops it back up. Credit never goes above the ceiling, refills included, which bounds what can ever sit unused at Bitrefill. When sales slow, the target shrinks and nothing new goes in.
- **Knowing the credit.** Bitrefill's balance is read every two minutes. In between, the estimate subtracts the price of every card ordered since and of every purchase being admitted or ordered. A drop in the balance that our own orders don't explain raises an alert, since it would mean someone else is spending the credit.
- **Purchases never count on the same credit twice.** Deciding whether a purchase can go ahead, and recording that decision, happen under one lock. A real card the credit can't cover waits as `waiting` before the customer is charged anything, and a refill starts at once. The purchase goes ahead when the credit lands, or is cancelled after a time limit, uncharged. If Bitrefill still answers "balance too low" when ordering, the already-admitted purchase waits as `restock` and is refunded in full if the credit doesn't come in time. Cards above the largest single order an admin allows are refused up front. Each account may have one purchase waiting, and a waiting purchase still needs the customer's balance and limits to cover it.
- **Refilling.** A refill asks Bitrefill for a USD credit deposit, paid 1:1 in USDC on Base. The deposit is refused unless it costs exactly the credit it buys and pays a well-formed address. NEAR Intents 1Click then swaps the treasury's USDC on NEAR into exactly that amount, delivered straight to Bitrefill's address. The quote is refused unless 1Click echoes back that recipient, those assets, that exact output and our treasury as the refund address, and unless it costs at most 1% more than the credit (the slippage buffer comes back to the treasury). Every step is saved before its side effect, and the treasury's transfer is saved signed before it's sent, so a restart resumes with the same bytes and never pays twice. Only one refill runs at a time, which the database enforces. A swap that fails, or a deposit Bitrefill doesn't credit, stops automatic refills until an admin resolves it in the panel. Two failed or refunded refills in six hours do the same.

**The treasury.** Refills are paid by a separate account whose full-access key the server holds (a function-call key can't attach the 1 yoctoNEAR that `ft_transfer` needs). It holds only what refills need. The contract decides how it gets that money, and only the owner can change it:

- **Automatic** (the default): the owner sets the treasury and a daily cap with `set_treasury`. `sweep_proceeds`, signed by the treasury itself, sends proceeds only to the treasury, only up to that day's cap, and puts both the proceeds and the cap back if the transfer fails. The server pulls exactly each refill's shortfall, so the treasury is nearly empty between refills.
- **Manual**: a cap of 0. Nothing is pulled; when a refill needs money, the panel and alerts say how much, and the owner sends it with `withdraw_proceeds(amount, to_treasury: true)`, which can only send to the owner or the treasury.

Switching modes is an owner transaction in the admin panel. The operator's key can do none of this: it can't attach a deposit, and the contract checks the caller anyway (`scripts/attack.ts`).

**What a compromise could cost.** With the server's keys an attacker could take what the treasury holds plus up to the daily cap of proceeds per day. The Cashback Reserve stays out of reach. Customer balances are only protected by their Spending Limits: the operator key can buy within them and confirm delivery without a card, which moves the Reference Price into proceeds, and the treasury key can then pull those proceeds (corrected 2026-11-11; an earlier version said customer balances stay out of reach). So the daily cap should stay near real daily sales, and the hard stop for a suspected server compromise is pausing the contract and setting the cap to 0. With Bitrefill's API key, they could spend the current credit, which never exceeds the ceiling. The owner can switch the treasury to manual at once, and every pull and refill is announced in the admin alert channel.

**Limits.** Refills run on mainnet only: NEAR Intents doesn't run on testnet, and testnet's test cards are free. The treasury must be registered on USDC before its first pull; `scripts/treasury.ts` does it and checks the pull, cap and failure paths live. Bitrefill caps our account at 15 purchases a day and its balance at about $2,000, and the server reads the remaining room from Bitrefill before each refill.

**Amended 2026-11-10 (stuck refill).** The first mainnet refill paid a 1Click quote 14½ minutes after asking for it, because the refill had to wait for the treasury in between; 1Click neither filled the swap nor refunded it at the deadline (`docs/incidents/2026-11-10-stuck-refill.md`). Refills now get the money first: the treasury holds the most a refill may cost (its amount plus 1%) and its gas before any invoice or quote exists, then the invoice, quote and payment run back to back, and a quote more than two minutes old is replaced, never paid. 1Click refunds from our own deadline, 15 minutes after the quote, and a swap still unfinished 20 minutes later is called stuck, before Bitrefill's 60-minute invoice expires. The quote must also echo delivery on Base and deposit and refund on NEAR. A stuck swap keeps being checked, and its alert says what to ask NEAR Intents support for: a refund to the treasury, not completion. When 1Click later refunds it, or finishes it and Bitrefill credits it, the refill closes the way the pipeline would have; only an unclear outcome waits for an admin.

**Amended 2026-11-10 (Base wallet).** A refill no longer swaps straight to Bitrefill's invoice address. 1Click delivers the USDC to our own Base wallet, and only once it's there does the server ask Bitrefill for an invoice and pay it from the wallet, within seconds. A swap that stalls or finishes late therefore lands with us instead of on an expired invoice, and USDC left in the wallet is used by the next refill first. The wallet has no private key: its address is derived from the treasury account through NEAR Chain Signatures, and NEAR's MPC network signs each Base payment when the treasury asks (about 0.01 NEAR each), so control of the treasury account, the server's key or the owner's wallet, is control of the wallet. Each payment's nonce and fees are fixed before signing and the signed bytes saved before sending, so a retry can only resend or replace it, never pay twice, and every signature is checked to recover to the wallet before anything is sent. A payment signed but not sent before its invoice is 50 minutes old is never sent. Base RPC (Alchemy) is required on mainnet, and the wallet needs a little ETH for gas, which the Monitor watches.

**Amended 2026-11-10 (no order refused for its size).** The largest-single-order setting is gone: a card bigger than the credit waits, uncharged, and a refill sized for it starts at once. Refills for purchases already waiting may lift the credit past the ceiling, since that credit is spent within minutes; idle credit still stops at the ceiling. A purchase may wait up to a day (it was 30 minutes), and the customer can cancel it until it's charged. Only a card bigger than Bitrefill lets our account hold is refused. The target now also covers the biggest card bought in the last week plus a refill's worth, and the refill line never sits below that card, so cards of the usual size buy at once. The mainnet daily refill limit is $300 (was $40). A contract change that would let each purchase fund its own card was considered and rejected: refunds must stay guaranteed, so Luckout money would still have to back every order in flight, and the contract would get riskier for no gain. Big orders are therefore limited by money on hand, which the server reports instead of refusing: it alerts when waiting purchases can't be refilled and why, when the day's pulls reach 80% of the owner's cap (with a cap to raise it to), and when a day's refills are far above the usual. The cap itself stays owner-set in the contract, so a stolen server key can never move more than it.


**Amended 2026-11-11 (Base float).** A float of USDC is kept ready in the Base wallet (an admin setting, $50 by default on mainnet; 0 turns it off). A refill pays Bitrefill from it in about a minute instead of waiting on the 1Click swap, which has taken 15 to 53 minutes and once stalled. When no refill is due and the wallet holds under half the float, a "float" refill swaps the rest back in through the same steps (funded first, quote checked field by field, saved before sent) and ends once the USDC lands; it never pays Bitrefill, a real refill always goes first, and one refill runs at a time as before. Because refills are now quick, the credit target drops from two days of sales to one while a float is set, so less money sits at Bitrefill, where it can never be refunded; the float itself stays ours. Holding the whole treasury on Base was rejected: proceeds are earned in the contract on NEAR, and the contract's owner-set daily cap is the main bound on a stolen server key, which nothing on Base would give. The float adds exposure equal to its size and is kept small.

**Amended 2026-11-12 (waiting purchases first).** A refill is sized to bring the credit back to its target plus what waiting purchases need. When the treasury can't fund all of that, an automatic refill is cut, before anything is signed, to what the treasury holds plus what it may still pull today. It's never cut below what the waiting purchases need or the $10 minimum, and an admin is told. So a waiting card goes through as soon as there's money for it, instead of waiting until the full top-up is covered. A refill an admin asks for keeps its amount.
