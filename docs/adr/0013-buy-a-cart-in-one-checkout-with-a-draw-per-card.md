# Buy a cart in one checkout, with a Draw per card

Customers want to buy several cards at once, as they can on Bitrefill. Bitrefill's `buy-products` takes a cart of up to 15 items in one invoice, any mix of products (checked 2026-11-18). Its guest limits count cards, not invoices, so a 15-card cart uses a Checkout Email's whole day. The first version ran each card of a cart as its own purchase. The Base Wallet pays one invoice at a time (8 to 12 s each on staging), so the last card of a full cart arrived minutes after the first. Every card also took Free Reserve separately, and a cart was checked card by card against reserve that later cards might no longer find. To bound that, the server allowed only 3 Lucky Buys in progress per account, which is too few for a cart.

So a cart becomes one **checkout**, and each of its cards stays a purchase of its own:

- **The contract admits a cart whole.** `purchase_many` admits every card, or none, in one call. Each card is admitted exactly as `purchase` would admit it, in order: the Balance and the daily Spending Limit are checked against the cards before it, the per-purchase limit against the card, and a Lucky card's Cashback Cap and lock come from the Free Reserve the cards before it left. Any refusal refuses the whole cart, and each card's `order_id` keeps a retry from admitting it twice.
- **The terms shown are the terms admitted.** `quote_many` prices a cart card by card the same way. A Lucky card that the reserve covers only in part plays that part, and the rest is at the normal price. A Lucky card that the reserve can't cover at all comes back Normal. The customer sees all of this before confirming, and admission plays exactly what they confirmed or refuses. The Lucky Amounts stay the contract's to compute (ADR-0004).
- **The reserve, not a count, limits Lucky cards.** The Cashback Cap is per card: 20% of the Free Reserve left at that point. A few big cards can lock most of the reserve until their Draws; that is accepted for now (amends ADR-0002). The owner's lever is still the Cashback Cap's share.
- **One invoice, one payment.** The server orders the cart as one Bitrefill invoice and pays it with one Base payment, no more than the sum of the admitted prices (ADR-0012). The invoice lists its orders in cart order with each one's product and amount, so every code is matched to its card.
- **Settled together, each card on its own.** `settle` confirms the delivered cards and fails the rest in one call. The Lucky Buys' Draws then run together in one callback, `on_draw_many`, in a later receipt. As with `confirm_delivery`, nothing that sees an outcome can undo a confirmation (ADR-0001, amended 2026-11-09). Each Draw keeps its own random value, `sha256(random_seed ‖ purchase_id)`, so cards drawn together are independent. A card the callback didn't draw is drawn by the next `settle` or by `retry_draw`, and every card keeps its own refund and its own Reclaim.
- **The server's key grows by two methods.** The server's function-call key may call only the methods it lists (ADR-0001). It now lists `purchase_many` and `settle` too. That needs a new key on each server account, and the old one is deleted. `purchase`, `confirm_delivery` and `fail_purchase` stay, so a server that doesn't use carts yet keeps working with the new contract.

Measured in the sandbox, a 15-card `purchase_many` burns 11 Tgas, and a `settle` of 15 Lucky cards burns 17 Tgas in all, 10 of them in the Draw callback. Both fit in the 100 Tgas the server attaches.

Rejected: one Draw for the whole cart (each card's Cashback would no longer match its own price and proof), and a limit on Lucky Buys in progress per account (it limits by count what is really limited by reserve money).
