# Build on NEAR with a chat agent and prepaid balances

Luckout is built for the NEAR hackathon (development ends 2026-11-14 23:59 UTC). One NEAR contract holds each customer's Balance in native USDC on NEAR (`17208628f84f5d6ad33f0da3bbbeb27ffcb398eac501a31bd6ad2011e36133a1`), their Spending Limits and Default Mode, the immutable Game Versions, the Cashback Reserve, and every Draw. Customers deposit with `ft_transfer_call` and may withdraw any unlocked Balance at any time. A Top-Up from any other chain is a NEAR Intents 1Click swap that lands USDC in the customer's NEAR account, followed by one deposit, so funds from several chains pool before any purchase exists.

Customers use two channels backed by one server. The Web App is where a customer connects their NEAR wallet, tops up, sets Spending Limits and Default Mode, browses and buys, and reads purchase history, Cashback, and Redemption Data. The Agent is a Telegram bot whose language model can search the catalog, check Balance, and buy; Telegram is chosen over X because voucher codes must be private and X write access is paid. A customer links Telegram to their NEAR account once from the Web App by signing a one-time code with their wallet; that link lives in the server database, not onchain. Every purchase from either channel is submitted by the server's NEAR account, which holds only a function-call access key restricted to the contract's purchase methods, so customers pay no gas and sign nothing per purchase. The contract enforces each customer's Spending Limits on every submission, so a fully compromised server can spend at most those limits. Raising limits and withdrawing need the customer's full-access signature. The server also sets the Reference Price; customers trust it to match Bitrefill, and every purchase records it onchain for comparison.

A purchase is either a Standard Purchase (the Normal mode) or a Lucky Buy. Fulfillment pays Bitrefill from the operator's prepaid Bitrefill account balance, so the voucher code arrives in seconds; the operator refills that balance and sweeps its proceeds in periodic batches. The balance is Bitrefill's USD store credit, topped up 1:1 with USDC, so the float carries no BTC exposure; purchases name it explicitly (`balance_currency: "USD"` through Bitrefill's MCP endpoint), because Bitrefill's REST API only spends the account's primary balance, which is BTC (see `docs/research/bitrefill-funding.md`). A Lucky Buy debits the Maximum Price from the customer's Balance and locks its largest possible Reference Discount from Free Reserve. When the voucher is delivered, the server confirms delivery onchain, and the same call runs the Draw and credits any Cashback to the customer's Balance, so the code and the result reach the customer together, seconds after they buy, with no reveal animation. The confirmation is submitted before its block's randomness exists, so the operator cannot see a result and then choose to declare the purchase failed instead. The random value per purchase is `sha256(env::random_seed() || purchase_id)`, so purchases confirmed in the same block draw independently; block producers can bias `random_seed` slightly, which is acceptable only because the Discount Cap and Spending Limits bound every Draw. A Standard Purchase debits the Reference Price with no Draw and no lock. If Bitrefill fails, the operator marks the purchase failed and the full amount debited returns to the customer's Balance with no Draw, so Bitrefill refund risk sits with the operator. If the operator neither confirms nor fails a purchase within 24 hours, the customer may reclaim the full amount debited.

Every Lucky Buy commits to an immutable Game Version; new economics need a new version. Only a Final Price below Reference Price is ever called a discount or win. Voucher codes are delivered in the Web App and in a Telegram direct message, and the Bitrefill order history is the recovery path. The contract owner account may pause new purchases, which never blocks confirmation, settlement, failure, reclaim, or withdrawal.

**Amended 2026-11-09.** The Draw no longer runs inside `confirm_delivery`. A full-access key could send `confirm_delivery` together with a second action that panics when the outcome is bad for the house; the whole transaction then reverts and can be retried until the Draw comes out the way the signer wants. `confirm_delivery` now marks a Lucky Buy as Drawing and schedules a private `on_draw` callback with gas the contract sets; the callback runs in a later receipt with that block's randomness and credits the Cashback. The confirmation can't be undone by anything that sees the outcome. If the callback ever fails (for example out of gas), the operator's next `confirm_delivery` or the customer's `retry_draw` schedules it again. The code and the result still reach the customer together: the server waits for the whole transaction, callback included, before replying. Purchases also start at 1 USDC, order ids are bound to their customer and price, and the Risk Premium is rounded up.

**Amended 2026-11-10.** The minimum purchase is now 0.5 USDC, down from 1 USDC, so small cards such as ₹50 Swiggy (about $0.56) can be bought. The floor exists because every purchase stores a record: a stolen operator key could fill contract storage with tiny purchases and block new ones (review finding C3). Halving it doubles how many records such a key could create within each customer's Spending Limits, and the alert on the contract's NEAR balance still catches it.

**Amended 2026-11-10 (ownership).** The owner can hand the contract to another account in two steps: `propose_owner` names it, and nothing changes until that account calls `accept_owner` with its own full-access key, so a typo can't give the contract away. The accounts whose keys the server holds, the operator and the treasury, can never become owner, and the owner can never be the treasury. On mainnet the owner is the operator's own wallet from the start; the admin panel shows a pending proposal and offers to accept it.

**Amended 2026-11-11.** `purchase` takes the Lucky Amount the purchase was accepted under (`lucky_amount`, optional). The contract plays exactly that amount, or refuses if the Cashback Reserve no longer covers it, so a purchase that waited for credit is never admitted to a smaller game than it was accepted for. Without it, the Lucky Amount is the most the reserve allows at admission, as before. A refunded purchase (failed or reclaimed) still counts toward that day's Spending Limit: restoring it would let a stolen operator key repeat purchase and failure without limit. The operator can never be the owner, the proposed owner, the treasury or the contract itself, and a Draw signed by the contract account's own key is refused.

**Amended 2026-11-11 (EVM wallets, ADR-0011).** A customer can also sign in with an EVM wallet. Their account is their lowercase `0x…` address, which is also their NEAR Intents account. Their Top-Up lands there, and an intent they signed moves it into their Balance. Withdrawing and changing their limits or Default Mode need their own EVM key's signature, checked by NEAR Intents, which then calls our contract's `on_auth`. Their withdrawals go only to that Intents account. NEAR-wallet customers are unchanged.

**Amended 2026-11-13 (Spending Limits).** New accounts start at $200 a purchase and $1,000 a day, up from $50 and $100. Existing accounts keep the limits they have; when a purchase is over one, the Web App offers to raise it on the spot (to the new defaults, or what that purchase needs if more) in one wallet signature. A customer can also turn either limit off. The Web App stores that as `u128::MAX` and shows it as "No limit"; the contract treats it like any other value. That customer has opted out of the bound in ADR-0009 and ADR-0011: a compromised server key could then spend up to their whole Balance, not just their limits. The wallet page says so in plain words before they save it.
